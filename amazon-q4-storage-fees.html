Amazon Q4 FBA storage fees 2026: the 3x peak and stacking surcharges

Every October, Amazon FBA storage roughly triples — standard-size monthly storage jumps from $0.78 to $2.40 per cubic foot (as of 2026, US). The costly part is not just the higher base rate: the peak monthly rate, the aged-inventory surcharge, and the storage utilization surcharge are three separate charges that can land on the same unit in the same month. This page explains why Q4 is so expensive and how to reduce your exposure. To see these fees applied to your own inventory, drop your settlement report into the free SettleClear tool.

Why Q4 storage roughly triples

Monthly FBA storage is charged per cubic foot of space your inventory occupies, and Amazon raises the rate for the fourth quarter. Standard-size storage goes from $0.78 to $2.40 per cubic foot — about 3.1x — while oversize goes from $0.56 to $1.40, about 2.5x. Dangerous-goods (hazmat) rates rise too. These figures are as of 2026 for the US; confirm current rates in Seller Central.

Q4 costs more than the rest of the year combined

Because the peak rate is so high, three months of Q4 storage can outweigh the other nine months put together. For one cubic foot of standard-size inventory left in stock all year:

  • Nine off-peak months (Jan–Sep) at $0.78: $7.02 per cubic foot.
  • Three peak months (Oct–Dec) at $2.40: $7.20 per cubic foot.

The takeaway: whatever you leave sitting in Q4 is your most expensive storage of the year, cubic foot for cubic foot.

The peak rate applies to the whole quarter

The Oct–Dec rate is not a mid-quarter event or a Black Friday spike — it applies to the entire period from October 1 through December 31. Storage is billed on your average daily volume, so:

  • Inventory sitting in a fulfillment center on October 1 is already being charged at the peak rate.
  • Units you remove mid-month still incur peak charges for the days they were present that month.
  • Sending inventory in "for the holidays" in early October means paying the peak rate on it for as long as it stays unsold.

The practical implication: the decision that controls your Q4 storage bill is made in September, not December.

Three surcharges that stack independently

The single biggest misunderstanding about Q4 storage is treating it as one fee. It is not. On the same unit, in the same month, up to three charges can apply at once, each calculated separately:

  1. Peak monthly storage — the $2.40/cu ft (standard) base rate above.
  2. Aged-inventory surcharge — an extra charge on units stored past 181 days, added on top of monthly storage.
  3. Storage utilization surcharge — an extra charge tied to how much space you use relative to how fast you sell.

They do not replace one another. A slow-moving, older SKU held through Q4 can be hit by all three simultaneously — which is how storage quietly turns a "profitable" SKU into a losing one.

Aged-inventory surcharge: the clock starts at day 181

A common piece of stale advice says long-term storage doesn't bite until 271 or 365 days. As of 2026, the aged-inventory surcharge starts at day 181. It is assessed on the 15th of each month, and it is charged on top of your monthly storage — not instead of it. The clock starts when a unit is received into FBA and does not pause for Q4, so units that crossed 181 days over the summer keep accruing right through the peak season.

Storage utilization surcharge: space vs. speed

The storage utilization surcharge is a separate penalty for holding a lot of inventory relative to how quickly you sell it. As of 2026 it applies only when both conditions are met: your average daily volume is at least 25 cubic feet, and your utilization ratio is greater than 22 weeks of supply on hand. Rates run roughly $0.44 to $1.88 per cubic foot, layered on top of everything else.

It does not apply to everyone. The surcharge is exempt for:

  • Your first 365 days selling on FBA.
  • Inventory that is under 30 days old.
  • Dangerous goods (hazmat).

Confirm the thresholds and current tiers in Seller Central, as Amazon adjusts these.

Fulfillment surcharges are separate from storage

Q4 also brings higher per-unit fulfillment charges. These are not storage fees, but they hit the same holiday orders, so it helps to keep them straight. As of 2026 (US), context on fulfillment includes:

  • A holiday peak fulfillment surcharge averaging about $0.32/unit, running Oct 15, 2026 through Jan 14, 2027.
  • The rate card rose about $0.08/unit on Jan 15, 2026.
  • A 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge (about $0.17/unit on average) was added Apr 17, 2026.
  • A low-inventory-level fee can apply when your days of supply fall below 28.
  • The referral fee is 15% for most categories, with a $0.30 minimum.

All of these change; verify current figures in Seller Central. The point for Q4 planning is that peak fulfillment and peak storage rise together, so a slow SKU pays more to store and more to ship during the same window.

How to reduce your Q4 storage exposure

  1. Audit before October. Identify SKUs approaching 181 days and slow sellers well ahead of the peak-rate switch on October 1.
  2. Send in only what Q4 demand justifies. Since the peak rate applies to average daily volume all quarter, over-stocking "just in case" is directly billable.
  3. Clear aging units. Liquidate, discount, or remove inventory nearing or past the aged-inventory bands — remember the clock never pauses for Q4.
  4. Watch your utilization ratio. If you're near 25 cu ft average daily volume and 22 weeks of supply, drawing inventory down can keep you under the utilization surcharge.
  5. Reconcile every settlement. Confirm the storage, aged-inventory, and utilization lines that were actually charged match what your inventory should incur — surcharges that stack are easy to under-count by hand.
