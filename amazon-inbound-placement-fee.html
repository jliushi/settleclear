Amazon FBA inbound placement fee 2026: rates and how to avoid it

The FBA inbound placement fee is a per-unit charge that applies when you send a shipment to fewer destinations than Amazon's optimized split. You choose how to split a shipment in Send to Amazon, and the fee that choice triggers is billed roughly 45 days after your inventory is received, on the quantity actually received. This page explains the three split options, the 2026 US rate card by size tier, and the tradeoff most sellers miss: the $0 "optimized" split usually raises your freight, so avoiding the fee is not the same as saving money. What the placement fee is

When you create an FBA shipment, Amazon can distribute your units across several fulfillment centers so stock sits closer to buyers nationwide. Splitting a shipment yourself into fewer destinations shifts that distribution work to Amazon, and the placement fee recovers that cost. The amount is per unit and varies by three things: the product's size tier, its weight band, and the destination region (West Coast destinations are the highest). These figures are as of 2026 for the US; confirm current rates in Seller Central, because Amazon adjusts them.

Two timing points matter for reconciliation. The fee is set the moment you pick a split in Send to Amazon, and the exact per-unit number is shown there before you confirm the shipment. It then bills about 45 days after the inventory is received, and it is calculated on the quantity received, not the quantity you shipped.

The three split options

Send to Amazon presents up to three ways to distribute a shipment. Which options you see depends on your product's size tier and on carton or pallet quantities.

OptionDestinationsPlacement feeNotes
Minimal split1 destinationFull per-unit feeFewest inbound shipments; highest placement fee.
Partial split2–3 destinationsReduced per-unit feeBulky products only since Feb 20, 2025; removed for standard-size items.
Amazon-optimized split5+ destinations$0Requires 5 or more identical cartons or pallets per item; spreads freight across regions.

The minimal split is the simplest to ship but carries the full fee. The optimized split removes the fee entirely, but it is only offered when you have at least five identical cartons or pallets of an item, and it deliberately spreads your inventory across multiple regions. That distribution is where the hidden cost lives, covered below.

What changed in 2026

Two changes are worth checking against your own SKUs:

  • The old "Large Bulky" tier was split into Small Bulky (up to 37 × 28 × 20 in) and Large Bulky. Small bulky prices are lower in every weight band, so an item you have long treated as large bulky may now qualify as small bulky. Measure the packed carton to confirm which tier applies rather than assuming.
  • Standard-size minimal-split rates rose about $0.05 per unit. It is a small per-unit change, but it compounds across high-volume SKUs.

Products and inventory that are exempt

Not every unit is subject to the placement fee:

  • Extra-large products pay no placement fee.
  • Inventory entering through Amazon Warehousing & Distribution (AWD) carries no separate placement fee.

If a meaningful share of your catalog is extra-large or moves through AWD, the placement fee may be a smaller factor in your inbound cost than the rate card alone suggests.

The honest tradeoff: $0 is not free

The optimized split shows a $0 placement fee, which reads like the obvious choice. It rarely is, because that $0 is bought by shipping your units to several destinations at once, often four, five, or more regions instead of one. Every additional destination is more freight: more cartons routed to more locations, and some of them far from your origin. The placement fee you avoided reappears, in whole or in part, as higher inbound shipping cost.

So the correct comparison is not "fee vs. no fee." It is total inbound cost per unit under each split:

  1. Read the exact placement fee for the minimal split in Send to Amazon.
  2. Get your freight quote for shipping to that single destination.
  3. Compare it against the optimized split: $0 placement, but freight to four, five, or more destinations.
  4. Add placement plus freight for each option and divide by units. Pick the lower total per unit, not the lower placement fee.

For a light, low-fee standard-size item, paying the minimal-split fee and shipping to one destination is often cheaper overall. For heavier or bulky items where the placement fee is large, the optimized split can win despite the extra freight. There is no universal answer, which is exactly why you should run the numbers per shipment.
