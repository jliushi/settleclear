Walmart Marketplace seller fees and true profit (2026)

Walmart Marketplace charges differently from Amazon: there is no monthly subscription, no listing fee and no setup fee — but a referral fee on every completed sale, plus fulfillment and storage fees if you use Walmart Fulfillment Services (WFS). This page explains those fees and how to work out true profit by hand. The SettleClear tool currently parses Amazon settlement reports (Flat File V2) only; multi-platform support including Walmart is on the roadmap. Amazon sellers can run their report now, and the manual math below applies to any Walmart seller today. All figures are as of 2026 — verify the exact rate for your category in Walmart Seller Center.

What Walmart charges — and what it doesn't

  • No monthly subscription, no listing fee, no setup fee. Walmart Marketplace has no recurring account cost.
  • Referral fee only on a completed sale. It is charged on the total sales price — item price plus shipping/handling plus gift wrap — not just the item price.
  • WFS fees apply only if you use Walmart's warehouses. Fulfill it yourself (seller-fulfilled) and you pay your own postage and labor instead, much like FBM on Amazon.

Referral fees by category

Referral fees range from 6% to 20% by category as of 2026; most categories fall in the 8%–15% band, and the default "Everything Else" rate is 15%. Some categories are tiered by price. Representative official examples:

Electronics Accessories15% on first $100, then 8%
Apparel & Accessories5% (≤$15) / 10% ($15–$20) / 15% (>$20)

Grocery8% (≤$15), then 15%
Baby & Beauty8% (≤$10), then 15%
Books, Home, Kitchen, Toys, Pet, Office15%

An unofficial per-item minimum referral fee (roughly $0.50–$1.00) is sometimes reported by sellers; treat this as unofficial and confirm against your own settlement lines. Note that Walmart assigns the category, not the seller — so a miscategorised item can be charged the wrong rate. You can dispute a referral fee you believe was charged on the wrong category. Always verify the current rate for your exact category in Walmart Seller Center.

WFS fulfillment fees

If you use Walmart Fulfillment Services, a per-unit fulfillment fee is charged on each unit shipped, scaled by weight:

Add-ons stack on top of the base fee: apparel +$0.50, hazmat +$0.50, retail price under $10 +$1, and oversize +$3 (or more). These rates are as of 2026; verify current WFS rates in Walmart Seller Center.

WFS storage fees

Storage is billed per cubic foot per month, where cu ft = L × W × H ÷ 1728 (inches). Rates are seasonal:

Period / conditionStorage rate (per cu ft / month)
January–September$0.75
October–December (peak), stored ≤30 days$0.75
October–December (peak), stored >30 daysadditional $1.50
Stored over 12 months$2.25

In other words, during the Oct–Dec peak, inventory sitting more than 30 days picks up an extra $1.50/cu ft on top of the base $0.75, and anything aged beyond 12 months is billed at $2.25/cu ft. Slow inventory in Q4 is where storage quietly eats margin.

The WFS settlement report

Walmart's WFS settlement report lists financial events by transaction type. Common types include: FulfillmentFee, ReturnProcessingFee, OffPeakSeasonStorageFee, PeakSeasonStorageFee, LongTermStorageFee, InboundTransportationFee, InventoryTransferFee, PrepServiceFee, disposal/removal fees, MultiChannelFulfillmentFee, Refund, Charge, DamageInWarehouse, LostInventory, FoundInventory.

  • Net Payable — the key money column. A positive value means the seller owes WFS; a negative value means WFS owes the seller. Damage, loss and refund lines are worth reconciling here.
  • Commission Rate — the referral rate applied to a line, useful for spotting a category charged at the wrong percentage.

This is structurally similar to Amazon's Flat File V2, where each row is a signed financial event — the same reconcile-every-line discipline applies. See how that works on the Amazon side in the settlement report guide.

2026 New-Seller Savings Program

For approximately Feb 2, 2026 – Jan 31, 2027, newly approved sellers have been offered a promotional discount: around 25% off WFS fulfillment, about 50% off storage (up to roughly $2,000), and up to about 75% off referral fees. Treat these as a promotion to verify, not permanent pricing — confirm current eligibility, percentages and caps in Walmart Seller Center before you build them into your margins.

The true-profit formula

For each item, revenue is not profit. Work it out as:

true profit = sale price − referral fee − WFS/shipping fees − COGS − returns allowance − advertising

  1. Start from the total sales price (item + shipping/handling + gift wrap) — that's the base the referral fee is charged on.
  2. Subtract the referral fee at your category's rate (mind tiered thresholds).
  3. Subtract WFS fulfillment and storage (or your own postage and labor if seller-fulfilled).
  4. Subtract cost of goods, a returns allowance, and any advertising spend.
  5. Reconcile against the WFS settlement Net Payable so nothing is missed.

How this compares to Amazon

The logic mirrors Amazon: a referral/commission percentage plus fulfillment and storage, with a per-line settlement to reconcile. The fulfill-it-yourself versus warehouse decision is the same trade-off Amazon sellers weigh between FBM and FBA — light, fast movers usually favor the platform's warehouse, while heavy or slow items often favor self-fulfillment.
