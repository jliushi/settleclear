Why is my Amazon payout less than my sales?

It is the most common panic in Amazon selling: you sold thousands, but the deposit that hit your bank is far smaller, and Seller Central does not explain the gap in plain language. Almost always the money is not lost — it is fees, refunds, advertising, and two timing mechanisms most sellers never had explained: reserve and deferred transactions. This page walks through all five, and shows where each appears in your settlement. To see the gap on your own numbers, drop your settlement into the free SettleClear tool — it separates fees, refunds, and reserve so the deposit finally reconciles.

Your deposit is not your sales

The single biggest misunderstanding is treating the deposit, or the order-notification "earnings", as revenue. The deposit is what is left after Amazon nets everything out. The settlement report's total-amount is what Amazon owes you for the cycle; the amount that reaches your bank can be lower still because of reserve. Here is the full waterfall.

The five reasons the deposit shrinks

  1. Amazon fees — referral (commission, ~15% for most categories), FBA fulfillment per unit, and storage. See how FBA fees work. These are permanent.
  2. Refunds — a return reverses the sale price and only returns part of the referral fee. If the unit never came back, you may be owed a reimbursement.
  3. Advertising — if Sponsored Products is billed on-account, it is deducted here; if billed to a card, it is missing from the settlement entirely, which makes profit look higher than it is.
  4. Reserve — money Amazon holds against future refunds, A-to-z claims and chargebacks. It is held from your total balance, not from any one order. It is not a loss; it releases in a later settlement.
  5. Deferred transactions — a sale is not released for payout immediately. Under Amazon's delivery-date-based policy, funds typically become available around 7 to 14 days after delivery. Recent sales sit in a deferred balance first, then move to available, so a strong sales week can look like a shrinking balance.

Reserve and deferred are timing, not loss

Fees and refunds genuinely reduce what you keep. Reserve and deferred do not — they change when you are paid, not whether. This is why a big sales spike can be followed by a smaller-than-expected payout: more of the balance is temporarily deferred or reserved, and it catches up in the next cycle or two. SettleClear surfaces the reserve amount on its own card and in an alert, so you can see exactly how much of the gap is timing rather than cost.

Why the numbers never match the dashboard

Your Business Report and the payout will not tie out, and that is expected. Business Reports are by order date and include cancelled orders; the settlement is by posted date, nets out fees and refunds, and excludes anything still deferred. Always reconcile against the settlement report, not the dashboard — it is the only Amazon document that matches the money in your bank. See the settlement guide for the field-by-field walkthrough.
