Amazon deferred transactions explained

If your Seller Central balance looks smaller than your sales — or a good sales week seemed to shrink your available funds — the usual reason is the deferred transactions system, not a mistake or a hold on your account. This page explains what deferred means, when the money releases, and how to read the report. It pairs with why your payout is less than your sales and how reserve works.

Total balance is not disbursable balance

Seller Central shows more than one number, and they are easy to confuse. Your total balance includes funds that are not yet payable; your available (disbursable) balance is what you can actually withdraw now. The gap between them is mostly deferred transactions and reserve.

What "deferred" means

A deferred transaction is a sale whose funds have not been released for payout yet. Amazon holds funds under a delivery-date-based policy: money typically becomes available around 7 to 14 days after the item is delivered. So the lifecycle is: a sale posts, its funds sit in the deferred balance, and after the delivery-plus-window passes, they move to the available balance. Only released transactions are included when a settlement pays out.

Why a sales spike can look like shrinking funds

This is the part that alarms sellers. When sales rise, the increase first shows up in the deferred balance, then transfers to available after the 7-plus-day window. So right after a strong week, more of your money is temporarily deferred, and the amount you can disburse can look lower than expected. It catches up in the next cycle or two. Amazon's own explanation is that the total funds are unchanged — they are just allocated differently between deferred and available.

How to read the deferred report

  1. Go to Payments → Transaction View in Seller Central.
  2. Set Transaction Status to Deferred transactions and update.
  3. Each row shows a deferral reason and an expected release date — the date the funds move to available.
  4. Cross-check against your settlement: the settlement pays only released funds, so recent deferred sales will appear in a later cycle.

Deferred is timing, not loss

Deferred funds are yours; they are simply scheduled for a later payout. The same is true of reserve. Neither reduces your profit — they change when you are paid. When you reconcile a settlement, work from the settlement report, which lists what actually paid out this cycle.
