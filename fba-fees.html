Amazon FBA fees explained (2026): every fee type and how it's calculated

Amazon takes more than 40 distinct fees out of a single settlement. Most sellers can name three of them. Below is every fee that touches your payout, how each is calculated, and the amount-description it shows up as in the settlement report. To see these applied to your own numbers, drop your report into the free SettleClear tool.

1. Referral fee (Commission)

A percentage of the item price — usually 8–15% depending on category, with a per-item minimum (typically $0.30). Charged on every unit sold. Appears as Commission under amount-type ItemFees. This is the single largest fee for most sellers.

2. FBA fulfillment fee (per unit)

A flat pick-pack-ship charge per unit, scaled by size tier and weight. A small standard item might be ~$3.20; oversized items climb fast. Appears as FBAPerUnitFulfillmentFee. If a SKU gets reclassified to a larger size tier, this jumps — and it's a common silent margin leak.

3. Monthly storage fee

Charged per cubic foot of space your inventory occupies, higher in Q4 (Oct–Dec). Appears as FBAStorageFee. Slow-moving inventory quietly erodes profit here.

4. Long-term / aged inventory surcharge

An extra charge on units stored beyond a threshold (typically 181+ or 271+ days). Appears as FBALongTermStorageFee or an aged-inventory surcharge. Often the first sign a SKU should be liquidated.

5. Refund administration fee

When you refund an order, Amazon returns most of the referral fee but keeps a refund administration fee (the lesser of $5.00 or 20% of the referral fee). Watch the net effect: a refund is revenue reversal plus a fee you don't get back.

6. Advertising (Cost of Advertising)

Sponsored Products and Brands spend. Critically, this is often billed to a card outside the settlement, so spreadsheets that only read the settlement miss it entirely — and overstate profit. SettleClear surfaces the Cost of Advertising lines that do appear so they aren't dropped.

7. Marketplace-facilitated tax (pass-through)

Sales tax Amazon collects and remits on your behalf. It appears as a collected amount and a withheld amount that net to roughly zero. This is not profit or loss and should be excluded from margin — a mistake many manual spreadsheets make.

The point: revenue is not profit
