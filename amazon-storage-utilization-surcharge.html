Amazon storage utilization surcharge explained

The storage utilization surcharge is the least-understood FBA storage cost because it is not about how long a unit sits — it is about how much space you occupy relative to how fast you sell. Hold far more inventory than your sales velocity justifies and Amazon adds a graduated per-cubic-foot charge on top of everything else. This page explains when it applies, the 2026 rates, who is exempt, and how to avoid it. Figures are as of 2026 for the US; confirm your ratio and the current rates in Seller Central. To see storage and every other fee on your own SKUs, drop your settlement into the free tool.

When it applies (two conditions)

The surcharge only triggers when BOTH are true, so many sellers never pay it:

  • Your average daily inventory volume is 25 cubic feet or more in a size tier, and
  • Your utilization ratio exceeds 22 weeks of cover. The ratio is your average daily inventory volume divided by your average daily shipped volume over the trailing 13 weeks — in plain terms, how many weeks of stock you are holding.

The 2026 rate tiers

The deeper your overstock (the more weeks of cover), the higher the per-cubic-foot charge, assessed monthly on top of base storage:

Who is exempt

  • Your first 365 days selling on FBA.
  • Inventory under 30 days old.
  • Dangerous goods.

It stacks with the other storage charges

This is the trap: the utilization surcharge, base monthly storage, the Q4 peak rate, and the aged-inventory surcharge are assessed independently and can all hit the same cubic foot in the same month. A slow, overstocked, older SKU in November can carry three or four charges at once — which is how storage quietly turns a profitable SKU into a losing one.

How to avoid it

  1. Keep weeks-of-cover under 22 in each size tier — sell through or send less.
  2. Stage buffer stock outside FBA (a 3PL or Amazon Warehousing and Distribution) and feed FBA in waves.
  3. Liquidate or remove dead stock before it drags your ratio up.
  4. Watch the opposite end too: cutting stock too far risks the low-inventory-level fee. The target is a healthy middle band.
